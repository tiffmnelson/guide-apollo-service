## Overview

The Australian Apollo Service provides hosted Apollo instances for Australian-based research groups and consortia. To access the service, you must submit an application form. Once approved (typically within 3–5 business days), you will receive credentials to access your Apollo Instance on the Nectar Research Cloud.

## Who Can Apply?

- Australian-based researchers
- Affiliated with a recognised Australian academic or research institute
- Principal Investigator (PI) must authorise the request
- Project Administrator must manage the instance

## Application Process

### Step 1: Prepare Your Application

Before applying, gather the following information:

- **Research group/lab name and website**
- **Principal Investigator details** (name, institution, email, phone)
- **Project Administrator details** (name, email) — must have command-line skills
- **Research description** (brief overview of your genome annotation project)
- **Organism types** you plan to annotate
- **Public availability** preferences (do you want genomes listed publicly?)
- **Grant information** (what funding supported your data generation?)

### Step 2: Complete the Application Form

Go to the [Apollo Service sign-up webpage](https://apollo-portal.genome.edu.au/sign-up) and complete the form with:

1. **Research Group/Lab Information**
   - Lab/consortium name
   - Website link (if available)
   - Suggested Apollo Host Name (e.g., `smithlab.genome.edu.au/apollo`)

2. **Principal Investigator**
   - Name, institution, email, phone
   - Acknowledgement of Terms of Use and Policies

3. **Research Description**
   - Brief description of your research objectives
   - Types of organisms you will annotate
   - Whether genomes can be listed publicly
   - Whether you plan to make genomes publicly available

4. **Project Administrator**
   - Name and email
   - Note: Must have command-line skills for data upload and management

### Step 3: Submit and Wait for Approval

Submit the form and wait for approval. The Apollo Service Team will review your application and contact you within 3–5 business days.

{% include callout.html type="note" content="Approval timeline is typically 3–5 business days. You will receive an email confirmation once your instance is created." %}

### Step 4: Receive Your Credentials

Once approved, the Apollo Service Team will provide:

- Your Apollo Instance URL (e.g., `smithlab.genome.edu.au/apollo`)
- Login credentials for the Project Administrator
- Instructions for accessing your instance

{% include callout.html type="important" content="The Project Administrator is responsible for managing user accounts, permissions, and data within the Apollo Instance. Only the Project Administrator can add/remove users and manage organism permissions." %}

## Accessing Your Apollo Instance

Once you have received your credentials:

1. Open a web browser and navigate to your Apollo Instance URL (e.g., `smithlab.genome.edu.au/apollo`)
2. Log in with your Project Administrator credentials
3. You are now ready to upload genome data and begin annotation

{% include callout.html type="tip" content="Bookmark your Apollo Instance URL for easy access. You can share this URL with collaborators (they will need login credentials to access non-public genomes)." %}

## Roles and Responsibilities

### Principal Investigator (PI)

- Authorises the request for an Apollo Instance
- Responsible for ensuring all users adhere to Terms of Use and Policies
- Accountable for the instance and its contents

### Project Administrator

- Manages the Apollo Instance on behalf of the research group
- Can add, share, or delete data for any organism
- Can add/remove user accounts and manage permissions
- Must have command-line skills for data upload
- Can request instance shutdown

### Users

- Can view and annotate genomes (permissions set by Project Administrator)
- Cannot add/remove other users or manage data
- Can export their own annotations
